(04) Notes

How it works

01 Getting a warning back through the fog in 300 ms FogLink · 2 min

A fog pileup starts with information that already exists: the first driver has stopped. The hard part is getting "something stopped 600 m ahead of you" to the right phones before the traffic behind arrives.

FogLink treats each carriageway as a line. A phone map-matches itself to "metres along NH-48, southbound", so "behind me" is just a smaller number on the same line. The Go relay never sees GPS. It keeps one latest position per anonymous ID for five seconds and forwards a stop event to every phone up to 2 km behind it.

Each phone then decides on its own whether to speak, from the deceleration its driver would need to stop in time. Through the live relay, event to spoken alert is 323 ms median. In simulation with every vehicle running it, 91% fewer vehicles are involved at 50 m visibility. At 10% adoption it barely helps, which is worth saying out loud.

02 Why I made a heat model obey physics Urban heat · 2 min

The goal wasn't just predicting land surface temperature across Delhi. It was answering "what happens if we plant more trees here?" A model that's accurate but learns nonsense, like more vegetation making a cell hotter, gives wrong answers to exactly that question.

So the XGBoost model has monotonic constraints: temperature can only fall as vegetation (NDVI) or moisture (NDWI) rises, and only rise with built-up area (NDBI). Constraining it this way also made it better, not worse. R² went from 0.61 with a random forest baseline to 0.71, with RMSE down from 3.1 °C to 2.6 °C.

SHAP then shows why each prediction came out the way it did. Built-up density is the biggest driver, and the cooling from vegetation flattens out above an NDVI of about 0.4.

03 Giving an AI agent a budget, but not the keys EzPC · 2 min

At ETHGlobal Mumbai our team built an agent that shops for a gaming PC on its own. It negotiates with other AI agents and pays for data with USDC. Letting an LLM spend money raises an obvious question: what stops it from spending too much?

Our answer was to never give it a wallet. The agent can only ask a separate Guard service to pay, and the Guard can only spend from an allowance the user approves once in their own wallet, enforced by Hedera itself. Its checks fail closed, every decision is written to a public audit log, and larger payments wait for a World ID check and a signature from the user.

The agent is free to be clever about prices, because it is never trusted with the money.
